# Pre-pilot audit — Alok Ingots Customer Portal

**Date:** 16 September 2026
**Audited commit:** `5b8b549` on `dev` (live server is at step 38, schema 0012)
**Scope:** everything in this repository, plus CI results from GitHub Actions
**Author:** senior-developer review, requested before inviting a real pilot customer

## How to read this

Findings are ranked **Critical** (do not invite a customer until this is done),
**Important** (fix soon; a customer may notice or it will hurt when something
goes wrong) and **Nice-to-have** (worth doing, nobody is harmed today).

Three words are used precisely throughout:

- **Verified** — proved on the live server, by a person or by a check that ran there.
- **CI-verified** — proved by the 272 tests in GitHub Actions, on every push. Real
  PostgreSQL, real migrations, but not the live machine and not a browser.
- **Unverified** — nobody has proved it anywhere. Usually because **Docker Desktop
  will not start on this PC**, so there is no local database and no local stack:
  about 175 of the 272 tests, and every end-to-end browser check, can only run in CI
  or on the server.

That gap is not a footnote. Between 15 and 16 September, three steps (36, 37, 38)
were merged and pushed while the test suite was **red in CI and nobody noticed** —
two tests had been calling `GET /api/staff/orders/{id}`, an endpoint that has never
existed. It was found only when the CI logs were read on 16 September. The code was
fine; the safety net was not being looked at. Treat every "CI-verified" below as
"true only while somebody actually reads the Actions tab".

## 2. Business-owner view — does it deliver the value?

**The core promise is met.** A customer can sign in without a password, see their
orders, see how much has shipped and how much is left, download the paperwork, and
open the carrier's own tracking page with the numbers ready to paste. That is the
"stop emailing us to ask" loop, and it closes.

**It looks credible.** Navy and white, the company name in the header, plain English
labels ("Shipped so far", "Still to ship", "Your documents"), no developer jargon on
any customer screen, real HTTPS on the company's own domain. Nothing about it reads
as a side project.

**What would embarrass the company in front of a customer, ranked:**

- **Critical — a customer who cannot sign in, and is told nothing is wrong.** If the
  customer's address is not on `NOTIFY_ONLY_EMAILS`, the portal still answers "Check
  your email" and sends nothing. The customer waits, emails to ask, and the first
  thing the portal ever did for them was fail silently. See C1.
- **Critical — losing a customer's documents.** Backups exist but never leave the
  server (C2).
- **Important — a Track button that does nothing for the next customer.** Only
  Evergreen is in the registry. A Maersk or Hapag-Lloyd shipment shows the numbers
  but no button (I9). Bucher is Evergreen, so this bites at customer number two.
- **Important — demo data in front of a real buyer.** "Demo Customer", "Second Demo
  Customer" and sample orders are what `seed.py` creates. Whether any exist on the
  live database is **unverified from here** (C3). Nobody can see another customer's
  orders — isolation is enforced and tested — so this is a credibility problem, not
  a leak.
- **Important — a scanned Mill Test Certificate that will not open.** Every document
  is served as `application/pdf` regardless of what it is (I2), so a JPG scan
  downloads mislabelled.
- **Nice-to-have — silence about what is still coming.** The customer sees only the
  documents that exist. Before anything is uploaded they get one general sentence;
  they cannot tell whether the invoice is late or simply not due (I8).

## 3. Customer view — walking Bucher through it

**Sign in.** They open the portal, type their address, press **Sign in with email
link**, and get "Check your email". The email arrives from `enquiries@alokindia.com`,
says the link works once and lasts 24 hours, and the link opens a page with one
button to press rather than signing them in automatically (so Outlook's scanner
cannot spend it). Good, and unusually well thought through.

- **Worry:** nothing tells them what to do if the mail does not arrive. No "check
  your spam folder", no "if it does not come in a few minutes, contact
  exports@alokindia.com". For a first-time user, that is the one moment they need a
  sentence and there is none. **Important.**
- **Worry:** "Sign in with email link" is clear, but the phrase *magic link* never
  appears anywhere a customer reads — good — while the email itself has not been
  looked at in a real inbox for rendering. **Nice-to-have.**

**Find the order.** They land on a list of their orders with the status beside each.
Clicking one opens Ordered / Shipped so far / Still to ship, the PO number, the grade,
then each shipment with a progress track, the shipment facts, photos and documents.

- **Clear:** the totals are labelled in buyer's language and the progress track shows
  five named steps, not a percentage.
- **Confusing:** **"Part shipped"** is Alok's internal idea. A German or Swiss buyer
  reading it will not know whether more is coming or the order is done. "Partly
  shipped — more to come" would say it. **Important.**
- **Confusing:** **"final shipment"** appears on the last lot without explaining that
  it means "nothing further will be sent against this order". **Nice-to-have.**
- **Missing:** no delivery address, no port of loading or discharge, no order date,
  no Shipping Bill number — all of which are on the Bill of Lading the buyer is
  holding. That is the planned step 39, and its absence is visible: the portal
  currently shows less than the paperwork. **Important.**
- **Missing:** no contact route. Nowhere on any customer screen is there an email
  address or phone number for a question. The whole point is to reduce emails, but a
  buyer who needs one anyway has to leave the portal to find out where to send it.
  **Important.**

**Tracking.** One button: *Track this container on Evergreen Line*, with the B/L and
container numbers beside it and a Copy button each, and a line saying the carrier's
page can be slow and asks for a number.

- **Honest, and worth defending.** Showing a vessel's live position would tell a
  transshipped customer their cargo is sailing the wrong way. Removing it was right.
- **But the customer is now doing the work.** They click out to ShipmentLink, paste a
  number into a form on somebody else's slow website, and read the answer there. It
  is defensible for a pilot; it is not "track your shipment", and the section heading
  says exactly that. **Important** — either the heading softens, or the paid carrier
  API moves up the roadmap.

**Language for a non-native reader.** Mostly plain and short. Two things to watch:
contractions in error text ("We couldn't find that order", "couldn't load") are
slightly harder to read than "could not"; and one message says "This is usually a
short connection problem", which is friendly but vague. **Nice-to-have.**

**Trust signals.** Real domain and HTTPS, company header, no third-party scripts, no
trackers, photos with the GPS stripped out, and a portal that never asks for a
password. That is a better trust story than most supplier portals. Against it: no
privacy note, no terms, no "who to contact", and no statement that the data shown is
their own. **Important** for an EU buyer (see I12).

## 4. Usability / UX

**Brand.** Followed. Navy `#000C2E` and white throughout, `color-scheme: light`, no
dark theme anywhere, and red `#BC0300` used only as a signal — errors, Cancelled, the
"Tick last shipment" warning. Checked in `frontend/src/index.css` and the three
stylesheets; the red is not used decoratively anywhere.

**Phone.** Every screen has been measured at 390 px and does not scroll sideways.
Caveat worth stating plainly: **no real phone has ever opened this portal.** Every
check has been an emulated browser, and on 16 September it emerged that headless Edge
on this PC had been laying out at 492–504 CSS px whatever width was requested — so
some earlier "390 px" checks measured nothing. The step 38 checks were redone
properly over CDP; older claims about phone widths are weaker than they read.
**Important.**

**Accessibility.** Better than typical, not audited:

- Real `<button>` elements with `aria-expanded` / `aria-controls` on the staff order
  rows; the progress track is an `<ol>` with a label saying "Step 3 of 5"; the photo
  lightbox is `role="dialog"` + `aria-modal`; the order table keeps table roles when
  it is drawn as cards.
- **Gaps:** photo alt text falls back to the *file name* when there is no caption
  (`IMG_2291.jpg` read aloud is noise); no skip-to-content link; focus is not
  demonstrably returned to the tile after the lightbox closes; no automated
  accessibility check anywhere in CI; nothing has been tried with an actual screen
  reader. **Important** as a group, **Nice-to-have** individually.

**Empty, loading and error states.** Consistently handled — every screen has all
three, with a **Try again** button rather than a dead end, and the staff screens have
a Start-here checklist when empty. This is the strongest part of the UX.

**Consistency.** One status pill component, one tracking component shared by both
halves, one date format (DD/MM/YYYY), one card style. The staff side now has two
different row idioms — the new collapsible order row and the older shipment/document
rows — which is defensible but worth watching. **Nice-to-have.**

## 5. Technical / code quality

### Security

**Good, and structurally so:**

- The read-only rule is enforced by architecture, not vigilance: the customer API is
  four GETs; everything that writes is under `admin/` behind `StaffUser`; a test
  asserts the customer half has no writes.
- Ownership is checked in the query for documents and photos, not after fetching.
- Magic links: 32 random bytes, stored only as a SHA-256 hash, 24-hour expiry, spent
  by a single conditional UPDATE (so two simultaneous clicks cannot both win), token
  carried in the URL **fragment** so it never reaches a server log, and redemption
  waits for a button press. Rate limited 3/inbox and 10/address per 15 minutes, with
  the same answer for every address so the portal cannot be used to discover who has
  an account.
- Passwords: PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 240k iterations, per-user salt — and dormant anyway.
- Uploads: extension **and** claimed type checked, 20 MB cap enforced while writing,
  stored under a random name so nothing user-supplied builds a path, photos opened by
  Pillow to prove they are pictures and re-saved without EXIF/GPS.
- No secrets in the repository: `.env` is untracked, `.env.example` holds only
  placeholders, and a scan for AWS keys and private-key headers found nothing.
- The audit log cannot be edited or deleted — a database trigger refuses UPDATE,
  DELETE and TRUNCATE even from a SQL prompt.

**Weak:**

- **No Content-Security-Policy** (I1). The Caddyfile says so in a comment and
  explains that `'self'` would now cover everything, since the third-party vessel
  frame was removed. It is a free win left on the floor. Also no HSTS in either the
  Caddyfile or the committed nginx site — certbot may add it; **unverified**.
- **Rate-limit state is in memory, in one process** (I6): lost on every deploy, and
  multiplied per worker if uvicorn is ever run with `--workers`.
- **Sign-in tokens cannot be revoked one at a time** (I7). Changing a password or
  deactivating an account retires all of them; there is no "sign out this device".
  12-hour TTL, held in `sessionStorage`, readable by any JavaScript on the page —
  acceptable for read-only order data, and it should stay a conscious decision.
- **Documents are not content-sniffed** the way photos are (N4) — staff-only, low
  risk, but a renamed file would be stored and served.
- **`doc_type` is free-form text** on upload (I10): a typo creates a new document
  category that the customer then sees.

### Error handling

Sound in the places that matter: the notification sender never raises (one bad
address cannot stop the rest), a failed deploy rolls the schema back and restores the
previous images, uploads are all-or-nothing, and `storage.resolve` returning nothing
gives the customer "This document has not been uploaded yet" rather than a 500.

The hole is **observability** (I5): there is no error tracking and no alerting. Two
loggers exist (`portal.magic_links`, `portal.notify`) writing to container logs that
nobody watches. If AWS SES starts refusing mail, or the API dies at 2 a.m., or the
15-minute notification sender throws every cycle, **nothing tells anybody** — the
only signal is a human opening the Messages tab or a customer complaining.

### Maintainability

Genuinely good, and better than most solo projects: layered backend with a stated
rule per layer (routers hold no logic, services hold no HTTP, settings read once),
`git mv` used so history follows, Alembic migrations with drills, one shared function
for anything both halves show, and `CLAUDE.md` recording not just what was done but
why — including decisions that were later reversed and the reasoning for the reversal.

Two blemishes: **`main` is still an empty anchor commit** although the portal has been
live for two days, which breaks the repo's own stated rule that `main` means "what is
deployed and working" (N6); and revision `0010` is reserved by an unmerged branch, so
the migration chain on `dev` reads 0009 → 0011 → 0012 and will need care if step 30
is ever merged.

## Verdict

**(a) Are we meeting the requirements the business wants?**

**Yes — six of eight fully, two partial, and both partials are honest.** A customer
can sign in without a password, see only their own orders, read a status that is
derived from reality rather than typed, download their paperwork, look at photos of
their material, and follow their container. The portal is live on its own domain
beside AlokCRM without having disturbed it, and the customer half genuinely cannot
write anything.

The two partials: **tracking** is a link-out rather than live positions, which was a
correct decision about transshipment and not a shortcut — but the screen still
promises more than it delivers by calling it "Track your shipment". **Documents** are
complete in code and proved in CI, but no document has ever travelled from a staff
upload to a customer download on the live server, and that must happen before a
customer is invited. Also worth correcting a belief held going into this audit:
**material photos are built**, and more carefully than most of the rest — previews,
format checking, and GPS stripped out of every full-size image.

**(b) Does it meet solid developer standards?**

**Yes, with two real weaknesses.** The things that are hard to retrofit are all
present: a security boundary enforced by structure rather than discipline, an audit
log the application itself cannot rewrite, database migrations with rollback drills,
a deploy script that backs up and restores itself, 272 tests on every push, and a
written record of why each decision was made — including the ones that were reversed.

The two weaknesses are both about **knowing when something is wrong**:

1. **Verification depends on a machine that is broken.** With no local database,
   175 tests and every browser check run only in CI — and for three steps nobody
   read CI, so a red suite shipped three times.
2. **Nothing watches production.** No error tracking, no alerting, no uptime check.
   The first person to know the portal is broken will be the customer.

Neither is a code-quality problem. Both are process problems, and both are cheap to
fix relative to what they protect.

### The five things to fix first

1. **`NOTIFY_ONLY_EMAILS`** — add the pilot customer, or they cannot sign in at all,
   and the failure is silent. *(C1)*
2. **A backup off the server, and a restore actually performed.** *(C2)*
3. **One real document uploaded and downloaded live**, plus a look at the live
   database for demo rows. *(C3, C4)*
4. **Content-Security-Policy and HSTS** — twenty minutes, and the comment in the
   Caddyfile already says what to write. *(I-a)*
5. **Close the verification gap** — get Docker working here or make the CI result a
   required read at every merge, and add the handful of frontend smoke tests that
   would have caught a broken screen. *(I4, I3)*

## Manual test checklist for the owner

Run this on **https://portal.alokindia.co.in** with a real browser. It takes about
twenty minutes and needs one staff login and the Bucher customer login. Tick each
line; anything unticked is a finding.

**A. Staff console**

1. Open the portal on a laptop. The sign-in card shows **one** box and **Sign in with
   email link** — no password box, and **no "Demo account" note**.
2. Sign in as staff by email link. The link arrives within a minute or two.
3. **Orders & shipments** — the list is slim rows, one per order, all shut. Click
   EXP-043: it opens to totals, the shipment, and the buttons.
4. Press **Collapse all**. Everything shuts. Refresh the page — the list is still
   tidy and the data is still right.
5. On the EXP-043 shipment press **Documents & photos**. Upload a real **Packing
   List** PDF. It reports success and the dot beside Packing List turns green.
6. Upload a **photo** of the material. It appears as a thumbnail.
7. **Customers & logins** — check the list. **Any "Demo Customer" or sample order
   must be gone.** Bucher's name, code and login address are correct.
8. **Messages to customers** — read what is waiting, sent or suppressed. Note whether
   anything says "not on the NOTIFY_ONLY_EMAILS pilot list".
9. **Change history** — your uploads from steps 5 and 6 are listed, with your name.

**B. Customer, on a laptop**

10. Sign out. Sign in as the Bucher customer by email link (use the customer's
    address — this also proves `NOTIFY_ONLY_EMAILS` is right).
11. The order list shows **only** Bucher's order. Nothing from any other customer.
12. Open EXP-043. Check: status, **Ordered / Shipped so far / Still to ship**, PO
    number, grade.
13. The shipment shows the progress track, the shipping line, vessel, container and
    B/L numbers, and the dates.
14. Press **Copy** beside the container number. Paste it somewhere — it is the right
    number.
15. Press **Track this container on Evergreen Line**. The carrier's page opens in a
    new tab.
16. The **photo** from step 6 is there. Click it — it opens full size. Press Escape —
    it closes.
17. Press **Download** on the Packing List. **Open the downloaded file** and check it
    is the right document and opens cleanly.
18. Try the address bar: change the order number in the URL to a different number.
    You must get "We couldn't find that order", never somebody else's order.

**C. Customer, on a phone**

19. Repeat steps 11–17 on a real phone. Nothing should scroll sideways, all five
    progress steps should be visible, and the Download and Track buttons should be
    easy to hit with a thumb.
20. Check the sign-in email on the phone: the link opens the portal and one press
    signs you in.

**D. The failure you most need to see**

21. Ask for a sign-in link using an address that has **no** login (for example your
    own personal address). You should get the same "Check your email" answer and
    **no email**. That is deliberate — but confirm you understand it, because it is
    exactly what a real customer sees when their address is missing from
    `NOTIFY_ONLY_EMAILS`.
